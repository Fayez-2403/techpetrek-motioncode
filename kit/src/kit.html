// 03 · the beat-sync method

Measure the song.
Then animate.

  1. Use the exact audio you'll post. My Short is a slower edit than the official "ultra slowed"; the tempo is different.
  2. Find the kicks: the steepest rise of the 35–160 Hz energy, at about 3 ms resolution.
  3. Fit the eighth-note pulse by robust least squares; hits more than 15 ms off are swing, so drop them.
  4. Mark the bars, the breakdowns (bass drops out) and the drops.
  5. Repeat whole phrases on bar lines with 4 ms splices; never stretch the song. A whole number of phrases loops seamlessly.
  6. Write the score in bars + eighths. Snap each hit to the measured onset within 70 ms.
  7. Moves land on the hit (start early). Accents fire 0–45 ms after it. Cuts take 0 ms.
  8. Sound effects come from the same cue file. Mix at −14 LUFS, true peak −1 dBTP or lower.

// 04 · the 10 motion rules

Studio, not slideshow.

  1. One clock. Every frame = f(t). Seeded randomness only.
  2. One object across every cut. Change what it is mid-move. Never crossfade copies.
  3. New composition every scene. Own layout, colour field, type treatment.
  4. Small overshoot. Spring damping 0.8–1.0; no default bounce.
  5. Draw lines, never fade them. Counters lock on the kick.
  6. Hits stack on one frame: flash, 2 inverted frames, shake, ring.
  7. Audio drives behaviour. The real waveform, the real bar; no EQ bars or orbs.
  8. One camera move per phrase. One quiet moment before each drop.
  9. Real demos: clicks, typing, loading, done. Label fictional brands.
  10. Safe zone + loop. Words in y 360–1420, clear of the right 120 px; premise on frame 0; end on frame 0.

// 06 · the perfect prompt (2/6)

STEP 1: MEASURE THE SONG (before any visuals)

  1. Decode the exact file. Find the kick attacks: the steepest rise of the 35–160 Hz envelope, at about 3 ms resolution.
  2. Fit the eighth-note pulse to those attacks with robust least squares. Drop hits more than 15 ms off the line; that's swing. Report the eighth, beat, bar and BPM, and the residuals.
  3. Bars are 8 eighths. Find each breakdown (where the bass drops out) and each drop (where it returns). Don't trust a BPM from the internet. If the song has two tempo readings (half or double time), tell me which you chose and why.
  4. If the song must be longer than the clip, repeat whole phrases on bar lines with 4 ms equal-power splices. Never time-stretch or pitch-shift.
  5. Save the measurements, the source hash and the method in a manifest.

STEP 2: WRITE THE SCORE IN MUSICAL UNITS

  • Write a score.json. Each section starts on a bar line, and each event is [bar, eighth], optionally snapped to a measured onset within 70 ms.
  • Use one section per bar. The first breakdown and drop go to the biggest reveal; the last breakdown is the quiet "one idea" moment; the last drop is the explosion before the title.
  • A script resolves the score into seconds (cues.json). Every visual and every sound effect reads only that file.
  • Moves that must land on a hit start early and finish on it. Reactive accents fire 0–45 ms after the hit. Hard cuts and colour flips take 0 ms.

STEP 3: BUILD IT DETERMINISTIC

  • Every frame is a pure function of time: drawFrame(ctx, t). No Math.random, Date.now or real-time loops; seed any randomness. Expose seek(t) so any frame renders on its own.
  • Draw vector frames straight at 1080×1920 (Canvas2D or WebGL). Blend 4 sub-frames across a 180° shutter for motion blur.
  • Encode H.264 High, yuv420p, BT.709 limited range with full colour tags, AAC 48 kHz, faststart. Mix at −14 LUFS with a true peak of −1 dBTP or lower.
  • Start from a deterministic engine: - pdoom-video (what this reel used) - HyperFrames (HTML) - Remotion (React)

// 06 · the perfect prompt (4/6)

STEP 4: THE MOTION RULES

  1. One continuous journey. Each creation physically becomes the next: - the hook object unfolds into the type, and the type stack becomes a browser; - the browser narrows into a phone while its content reflows; - the app's result card opens into a dashboard, and the card's bars become the chart's bars; - a chart bar rises into a product, which shatters into modules; - the modules build a character, whose world folds into a 3D city; - the city contracts into a record, which collapses into the hook object; - that object explodes into every scene, and the scenes fill the letters of the title. Never crossfade two copies of a thing. Move one object and change what it is mid-move.
  2. Every section gets its own composition, colour field and type treatment. Use masks, split-flaps, vertical type, elastic width, 3D billboards and audio-reactive letters. Never repeat "title on top, card in the middle".
  3. Use real springs. Damping ratio 0.8–1.0 (about 1% overshoot); no default bounce or elastic easing.
  4. Draw lines, don't fade them in. Counters count up, then lock on the kick. Hits stack on one frame: flash, 2 inverted frames, shake, ring.
  5. Audio drives behaviour, not decoration. Draw the song's real waveform and the bar's measured bass. No EQ bars, no pulsing orbs, no generic particles or gradients.
  6. One camera move per phrase, never per beat. Hold one quiet moment before each drop.
  7. Make it real. Show actual animated demos (navigation, cursor clicks, typing in an input, loading, done). Label invented brands and data as fictional.
  8. Show the process. Add a small code line at the bottom of every scene: that scene's real code, the clock and the eighth notes of the bar.
  9. Safe zone. Every word sits between y 360 and 1420 and clear of the right 120 px of the 1080×1920 frame. Headlines are 90 px or more, labels 24 px or more.
  10. Engagement. The premise is readable on frame 0. There's a payoff every bar. The CTA is big and holds at least 2 s. The last 0.25 s collapse back into frame 0, so the reel loops.

STEP 5: QUALITY CONTROL (before you say it's done)

  1. Show me a shot list with exact times, then stills at key frames.
  2. Make a contact sheet every 0.5 s and a strip at 0.05 s spacing around every cut. Look for dead air, text collisions, pops at hand-offs and anything that looks generic. Fix the three worst problems, then re-check.
  3. Determinism: render a set of times forwards and in reverse; the frames must match byte for byte.
  4. Render a low-res animatic first, then ask me before the full 60 fps render.
  5. Check the export: resolution, frame count, duration, colour tags, loudness, and the audio-to-cue offset (it should be 0 ms). Pull frames from the encoded MP4 and look at them.

STEP 6: DELIVER

  • The reel MP4 (and the 16:9 cut if asked), a cover frame, the shot list, and short notes on any deviation from this brief.
  • Also give me: a caption with the CTA, a pinned comment, and the credit line for the music.

// 07 · export + posting checklist

Before you post

  • 1080×1920, 60 fps, H.264 High, yuv420p, BT.709 limited range, full colour tags, AAC 48 kHz, faststart.
  • Every word in y 360–1420 and clear of the right 120 px. Headlines 90 px or more, labels 24 px or more.
  • Determinism: render a set of times forwards and in reverse; the frames must match exactly.
  • A contact sheet every 0.5 s, plus a strip at 0.05 s around every cut. Fix dead air and pops.
  • Pull frames from the final MP4 and look at them; check the audio-to-cue offset is 0 ms.
  • Music: add the track from Instagram's music library at your edit's start and lower the original audio; otherwise the reel may be muted.
  • Cover: pick a frame with the hook and the title in the safe zone.
  • Caption: the premise in line 1, one CTA keyword, 3–5 hashtags. Pin a comment with the keyword.
